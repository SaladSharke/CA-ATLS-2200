The color and typography choices were inspired by the brutalist architecture of the ATLAS 
building. The base color was sampled from a photo of the ATLAS building, and a palette was 
derived from that using a palette generator. We picked a palette with greens inspired by a 
proposition for an official City of Boulder color palette. The browns and greens feel “authentic” 
because they were directly inspired by the ATLAS building (which is where the BTU lab resides) 
and the colors of the mountains behind CU. Accessibility was considered in every step of 
planning colors and font choice; every single color in our palette was run through the WCAG 
tester against our chosen background color to check for at least AA contrast levels, and the font 
was chosen for being easy-to-read while still being professional. Creating good contrast without 
ruining the theme of the website was difficult, there were several times where we had to start 
with a completely new palette or base color because the ones we were working with had 
obscenely low (sometimes as low as 1.5) contrast. I personally was shocked at how fine a line 
had to be walked between artistic expression and accessibility, but the lesson learned there is to 
simply plan for accessibility as you’re coming up with concepts for a design (which is the 
practice we followed during this project, and it worked in the end). This system of development 
might be able to be refined a bit more by starting with known contrasting colors and accessible 
design, instead of picking whatever we want and reworking it into being accessible. Trying to 
retrofit existing designs into accessibility isn’t super flexible, and starting with accessible options 
and retooling them to be more artistic (effectively going the opposite way) would make things a 
bit easier.
